## Data Findings
- Service type: Express was delayed most often, with a delay rate of "0.4897" in the output table, compared with Standard at "0.2056" and Economy at "0.0857".
- Hub tier: the delay rate rose from "0.0400" at Tier 1 to "0.4400" at Tier 3, showing a sharp increase in risk as the hub tier worsened.
- Distance band: delay rate increased from "0.0219" in the 0-200 km band to "0.8523" in the 601+ km band, indicating a strong deterioration in on-time performance with longer distances.
- Delivery-hours correlation: the three strongest relationships with `delivery_hours` were `promised_hours` at "+0.8372", `distance_km` at "+0.4013", and `n_stops` at "+0.3247" when ranked by absolute correlation in `part_a_correlations.csv` after excluding `delivery_hours` itself.
- Log transform: `declared_value_inr` was log-transformed because the filled column was heavily right-skewed, with a mean of "1533.0542" and median of "1121.5000" before the transform, while the log version had a mean of "7.0277" and median of "7.0224", which is far more balanced for modeling.

## Model results (regression)
- Baseline performance: the validation baseline R² was "-0.0212", which means the constant-mean model was worse than a horizontal line at the mean; in practice, it barely predicted anything useful and was a weak benchmark.
- Validation winner: the best model on validation was `linear_regression`, with RMSE of "4.8912" hours, slightly ahead of `gradient_boosting` at "4.9165" hours and well ahead of the mean baseline at "18.8552" hours.
- Tree-depth bias/variance: the depth-1 model had train RMSE "11.6334" and validation RMSE "12.1324" (gap "0.4989"), which is a classic high-bias, underfit model. Depths 2, 3, and 4 stayed similarly biased with gaps of "1.0428", "1.1738", and "1.2897" respectively. The high-variance regime begins at depth 8 with a gap of "3.5025" between train RMSE "3.3360" and validation RMSE "6.8385", and it gets worse at depth 12 (gap "6.0560") and unlimited depth (gap "7.7890"). Depth 6 is the middle ground and was close to the best trade-off.
- Learning-rate choice: the best learning rate was "0.1", with validation RMSE "4.9165" and R² "0.9306". A rate of "0.01" was too slow, giving RMSE "10.0018" and R² "0.7127", which means it underfit and learned too little. A rate of "1.0" was too aggressive, with RMSE "7.3701" and R² "0.8440", implying overshooting and unstable updates.
- Cross-validation check: the CV ranking disagreed with validation, because `gradient_boosting` had mean R² "0.9352" versus `linear_regression` at "0.9261". I would still ship `gradient_boosting` if asked to choose a single model, because it has the stronger cross-validated signal, slightly better average R², and more consistent performance across folds, even though `linear_regression` won the single validation split by a very small margin.

## Model results (classification)
- Blind baseline: the blind model predicting every validation row as on time achieved accuracy "0.7667" because it got the majority class right, but it is useless for delay detection because it predicted zero delayed shipments and therefore had precision "0.0000", recall "0.0000", and F1 "0.0000" despite a seemingly acceptable accuracy score.
- Noise columns: the five noise columns had a combined random-forest importance share of "0.2282" (22.82%), which is not negligible but still modest relative to the signal features. All five noise features fell outside the top 7 by F-score: `label_print_batch` rank "8", `scanner_build_no` rank "17", `tape_roll_no` rank "13", `sticker_serial_mod` rank "14", and `shelf_slot_no` rank "16".
- The `k` table: `SelectKBest` reached its best validation F1 at `k = 7` with "0.8333". The other values were lower: `k = 5` gave "0.6988", `k = 10` gave "0.7857", `k = 12` gave "0.8148", and `k = 17` gave "0.8101". This shows that moderate feature reduction was helpful, but trimming too aggressively or keeping too many noisy features reduced performance.
- Tuning check: tuning did not beat the untuned forest on validation. The untuned forest with the same 7 selected features had validation F1 "0.8333", while grid search reached "0.7750" and randomized search reached "0.7848". On a 180-row validation slice, the score differences are small enough that tuning noise can easily dominate the result, so a strong default baseline is often a better choice than over-optimizing on a modest validation set.
- Threshold choice: the final classifier used threshold "0.5" because it is the highest threshold that still keeps validation recall at or above "0.80" while also preserving good precision. At threshold "0.5", precision was "0.7955", recall was "0.8333", and F1 was "0.8140"; lowering the threshold to "0.1" boosted recall to "0.9762" but crushed precision to "0.4824", while raising it to "0.6" improved precision to "0.8889" but cut recall to "0.7619".
- Test performance: on the held-out test set, the chosen model reached precision "0.6889", recall "0.7561", and F1 "0.7209". The recall declined from the validation recall of "0.8333" to the test recall of "0.7561"; this is a real drop, but it is also honest given the test set has only "41" delayed parcels, so a few misses move the rate noticeably. In the test set, the model caught "31" of those "41" delayed parcels and missed "10".
